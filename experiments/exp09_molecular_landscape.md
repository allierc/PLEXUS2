## The question

How does the crowd of MPM bodies move in the best run, and how does that differ from a real cytoplasm at this scale?

- 2026-11-14 20:20 -- In the best run (it04, the deep slab) each body is caged by its neighbours and escapes by collisions: the Langevin bath gives every body a thermal speed (about 0.17 nm per frame for the 12 nm ones) that it keeps for 20 frames, the overlap penalty turns it at every contact, and the result is a wander that grows as the square root of time, 14 nm for the ribosomes to 35 nm for the smallest globules in 200 us; nothing but excluded volume holds the picture together. A real cytoplasm differs in three ways: its molecules are overdamped (no remembered velocity, so no visible strides), its diffusion falls with radius rather than with mass, and most of its volume fraction is small proteins and water that this rig does not carry. The change this points to: a calmer bath at the same diffusion (it05), then the membrane with proteins that keep their shape (it06) in the deep slab.

- 2026-11-15 14:36 (at the stop) -- In the best runs each body is still a caged elastic blob moved by the Langevin bath and turned by the overlap penalty, as on 2026-11-14; what changed on 2026-11-15 is what the bodies are and what joins them. Shape is free: any body of 27 to 343 points keeps its radius of gyration at 1.00 for 1,000 frames whatever its lobes (it10). Joining is not: a negative contact tension makes a body creep over its partner at a rate that grows as the body shrinks (a 27-point thread element is torn by frame 500 at -0.03, a 190-point receptor head is untouched), so what must stay joined for the whole movie is tied by springs at a rest length (apical_junction: chains in it12, an ordered array in it13, two facing membranes in it15), and a tension is kept for docking between large bodies (Y bodies on receptors, it11; heads on thick filaments, it13). A real cell differs in the same three ways as before, and in a fourth: its bonds form and break, where these springs are set once at the first substep and never change.

## The model: one sentence, one operator

A slab of cytoplasm 192 x 108 nm wide and 52 nm deep, seen face on (16:9), filled to 26 % of its volume with 408 bodies.

| the sentence | operator | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| the molecules are soft solid bodies of different sizes and shapes | `seed_positions[from_points_ragged]` (NEW variant of `from_points`, adhesion_ops.py) + elastic MPM material | a table of points per body, padded with NaN rows: each body takes its own count, 27 to 343 points at 2 nm per point; Young's modulus 20, no relaxation, so a body keeps its shape |
| they jostle | `thermal_bath[langevin]` (exp37's) | on every material point, every MPM substep: a drag -friction x velocity and a random kick sized so the body fluctuates at thermal energy kT |
| they cannot overlap | `pair_potential[cell_contact]` | the overlap penalty between the volume fractions of two bodies; its contact tension per pair of kinds (negative = sticky) is 0 unless an iteration sets it |
| they stay in view | `active_force[box_slab]` (NEW variant of `active_force[layer_slab]`, flatten_ops.py) | soft walls on the six faces of the slab: acceleration = wall stiffness x the distance a point is beyond a face |
| (from it10) some molecules are long: filaments and threads | the same seed; `chains` in the tool | a chain is a row of block-shaped bodies seeded one point spacing apart, straight (8 nm subunits) or meandering (6 nm elements); no bond operator: what holds it is the next line |
| (from it10) some molecules bind others | `pair_potential[cell_contact]`, its `contact_surface_tension` matrix | a negative tension between two kinds makes their contact area pay: -0.05 between a chain's own subunits holds the chain, -0.05 between two kinds docks one on the other. At -0.2 a body spreads over its partner (it02, smoke 1 of it10) |
| the mechanics | the MPM substep block: `mpm_strain`, `mpm_scatter`, `mpm_grid_update[warp_pp_balanced]`, `mpm_gather` | each body on its own block of the grid (`per_parent`), 100 substeps of 0.01 per frame |

The six kinds of body (length unit 10 nm; one material point = 8 nm^3, about 6.5 kDa of protein):

| kind | points | shape | size | fill | bodies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ribosome | 343 | two unequal lobes | 22 x 15 nm | violet #9B5DE5 | 28 |
| chaperonin | 125 | barrel | 12 x 14 nm | blue #4D96FF | 40 |
| rod | 125 | rod | 9 x 23 nm | pink #F15BB5 | 40 |
| trimer | 125 | three lobes in a triangle | 15 nm across | gold #E9C46A | 40 |
| enzyme | 64 | globule | 10 nm | teal #2A9D8F | 90 |
| small | 27 | globule | 7 nm | lime #8AC926 | 170 |

Both new variants are tested in `tests/test_exp09_landscape_ops.py` (run on the job's GPU before every smoke): a table with no NaN row seeds exactly what `from_points` seeds; a padded table gives each body its own count and volume per point; the walls push only what is beyond a face.

**Deliberately absent.** Molecules below about 170 kDa (fewer than 27 points at 2 nm), water and ions, hydrodynamics, any chemistry. A body is a continuum blob, not atoms: this is a picture of crowding, not molecular dynamics.

## Decisions

- 2026-11-14: the scene is a thin slab seen face on, not a ball of molecules: a cross-section needs every body visible, four bodies deep gives about 85 % of the frame covered.
- 2026-11-14: length unit 10 nm, 2 nm per material point (the options: 1 nm per point shows 20 kDa proteins but needs 8x the points; 2 nm keeps 35,000 points and shows everything from 170 kDa up).
- 2026-11-14: time unit 0.2 us, set by reading the 12 nm body's free diffusion (kT / (friction x mass) = 0.002 length^2 per time unit) as 1 um^2/s; 1,000 frames = 0.2 ms. The bath's diffusion falls as 1 / mass, a real one as 1 / radius, so the 7 nm bodies are about 2.7x too fast against the 12 nm ones.
- 2026-11-14: friction 0.05 per time unit, i.e. a velocity memory of 20 frames, for smooth paths in a 250-frame movie; a real molecule forgets its velocity in picoseconds. Kept as the movie's choice, stated on every why text.
- 2026-11-14: elastic bodies with no surface tension (exp05/06/08 use viscoelastic cells with tension, which round up): a molecule must keep its shape.
- 2026-11-14: no `frame_rotation` wrapper: it turns the whole set about its centroid inside the substep, and a thin slab turned at random leaves the world box of the contact lattice.
