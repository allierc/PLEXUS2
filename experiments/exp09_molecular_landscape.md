## The question

How does the crowd of MPM bodies move in the best run, and how does that differ from a real cytoplasm at this scale?

- 2026-11-14 20:20 -- In the best run (it04, the deep slab) each body is caged by its neighbours and escapes by collisions: the Langevin bath gives every body a thermal speed (about 0.17 nm per frame for the 12 nm ones) that it keeps for 20 frames, the overlap penalty turns it at every contact, and the result is a wander that grows as the square root of time, 14 nm for the ribosomes to 35 nm for the smallest globules in 200 us; nothing but excluded volume holds the picture together. A real cytoplasm differs in three ways: its molecules are overdamped (no remembered velocity, so no visible strides), its diffusion falls with radius rather than with mass, and most of its volume fraction is small proteins and water that this rig does not carry. The change this points to: a calmer bath at the same diffusion (it05), then the membrane with proteins that keep their shape (it06) in the deep slab.

- 2026-11-15 14:36 (at the stop) -- In the best runs each body is still a caged elastic blob moved by the Langevin bath and turned by the overlap penalty, as on 2026-11-14; what changed on 2026-11-15 is what the bodies are and what joins them. Shape is free: any body of 27 to 343 points keeps its radius of gyration at 1.00 for 1,000 frames whatever its lobes (it10). Joining is not: a negative contact tension makes a body creep over its partner at a rate that grows as the body shrinks (a 27-point thread element is torn by frame 500 at -0.03, a 190-point receptor head is untouched), so what must stay joined for the whole movie is tied by springs at a rest length (apical_junction: chains in it12, an ordered array in it13, two facing membranes in it15), and a tension is kept for docking between large bodies (Y bodies on receptors, it11; heads on thick filaments, it13). A real cell differs in the same three ways as before, and in a fourth: its bonds form and break, where these springs are set once at the first substep and never change.

## The model: one sentence, one operator

A slab of cytoplasm 192 x 108 nm wide and 52 nm deep, seen face on (16:9), filled to 26 % of its volume with 408 bodies.

| the sentence | operator | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| the molecules are soft solid bodies of different sizes and shapes | `seed_positions[from_points_ragged]` (NEW variant of `from_points`, adhesion_ops.py) + elastic MPM material | a table of points per body, padded with NaN rows: each body takes its own count, 27 to 343 points at 2 nm per point; Young's modulus 20, no relaxation, so a body keeps its shape |
| they jostle | `thermal_bath[langevin]` (exp37's) | on every material point, every MPM substep: a drag -friction x velocity and a random kick sized so the body fluctuates at thermal energy kT |
| they cannot overlap | `pair_potential[cell_contact]` | the overlap penalty between the volume fractions of two bodies; its contact tension per pair of kinds (negative = sticky) is 0 unless an iteration sets it |
| they stay in view | `active_force[box_slab]` (NEW variant of `active_force[layer_slab]`, flatten_ops.py) | soft walls on the six faces of the slab: acceleration = wall stiffness x the distance a point is beyond a face |
| (from it10) some molecules are long: filaments and threads | the same seed; `chains` in the tool | a chain is a row of block-shaped bodies seeded one point spacing apart, straight (8 nm subunits) or meandering (6 nm elements); no bond operator: what holds it is the next line |
| (from it10) some molecules bind others | `pair_potential[cell_contact]`, its `contact_surface_tension` matrix | a negative tension between two kinds makes their contact area pay: -0.05 between a chain's own subunits holds the chain, -0.05 between two kinds docks one on the other. At -0.2 a body spreads over its partner (it02, smoke 1 of it10) |
| the mechanics | the MPM substep block: `mpm_strain`, `mpm_scatter`, `mpm_grid_update[warp_pp_balanced]`, `mpm_gather` | each body on its own block of the grid (`per_parent`), 100 substeps of 0.01 per frame |

The six kinds of body (length unit 10 nm; one material point = 8 nm^3, about 6.5 kDa of protein):

| kind | points | shape | size | fill | bodies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ribosome | 343 | two unequal lobes | 22 x 15 nm | violet #9B5DE5 | 28 |
| chaperonin | 125 | barrel | 12 x 14 nm | blue #4D96FF | 40 |
| rod | 125 | rod | 9 x 23 nm | pink #F15BB5 | 40 |
| trimer | 125 | three lobes in a triangle | 15 nm across | gold #E9C46A | 40 |
| enzyme | 64 | globule | 10 nm | teal #2A9D8F | 90 |
| small | 27 | globule | 7 nm | lime #8AC926 | 170 |

Both new variants are tested in `tests/test_exp09_landscape_ops.py` (run on the job's GPU before every smoke): a table with no NaN row seeds exactly what `from_points` seeds; a padded table gives each body its own count and volume per point; the walls push only what is beyond a face.

**Deliberately absent.** Molecules below about 170 kDa (fewer than 27 points at 2 nm), water and ions, hydrodynamics, any chemistry. A body is a continuum blob, not atoms: this is a picture of crowding, not molecular dynamics.

## Decisions

- 2026-11-14: the scene is a thin slab seen face on, not a ball of molecules: a cross-section needs every body visible, four bodies deep gives about 85 % of the frame covered.
- 2026-11-14: length unit 10 nm, 2 nm per material point (the options: 1 nm per point shows 20 kDa proteins but needs 8x the points; 2 nm keeps 35,000 points and shows everything from 170 kDa up).
- 2026-11-14: time unit 0.2 us, set by reading the 12 nm body's free diffusion (kT / (friction x mass) = 0.002 length^2 per time unit) as 1 um^2/s; 1,000 frames = 0.2 ms. The bath's diffusion falls as 1 / mass, a real one as 1 / radius, so the 7 nm bodies are about 2.7x too fast against the 12 nm ones.
- 2026-11-14: friction 0.05 per time unit, i.e. a velocity memory of 20 frames, for smooth paths in a 250-frame movie; a real molecule forgets its velocity in picoseconds. Kept as the movie's choice, stated on every why text.
- 2026-11-14: elastic bodies with no surface tension (exp05/06/08 use viscoelastic cells with tension, which round up): a molecule must keep its shape.
- 2026-11-14: no `frame_rotation` wrapper: it turns the whole set about its centroid inside the substep, and a thin slab turned at random leaves the world box of the contact lattice.
- 2026-11-14: soft walls as a variant of `active_force[layer_slab]`, not of `plate_confine`: the registry refuses a variant that changes its contract's kind (plate_confine is structural, a force is an exchange), and projecting MPM points moves material without its strain knowing.
- 2026-11-14: the simulation jobs pass `--no-viz --no-describe` as exp06 and exp08 do; the movie is drawn by the after-job, the caption by the devcontainer's VLM once the step is filed.

## Findings

1. **The rig is healthy as first written.** 5/5 unit tests on the H100; in every run all positions finite, no point more than 0.1 nm beyond a wall, and every body of the crowd keeps its radius of gyration within 1 % of its start over 1,000 frames. Cost: 1.8 s per frame for 408 bodies (35,000 points), 2.9 s for 705 (60,000 points): a 1,000-frame run is 30 to 50 minutes.
2. **The crowd mixes.** In 200 us the centres wander 13 nm (the 343-point ribosomes), 16-22 nm (the 125-point bodies) and 33 nm (the 27-point globules) from their start, RMS: one to four of their own diameters. At frame 500 they are at 70 % of those values, so the wander grows as the square root of time: diffusion, not a drift.
3. **Depth fills the frame, the volume share does not need to rise** (it04 against it01): at the same 26 % a slab 90 nm deep leaves almost no bare ground where 52 nm leaves about a sixth.
4. **One sticky kind condenses inside the crowd** (it03): with a contact tension of -0.15 between two three-lobed complexes and 0 for every other pair, the share of them with a partner within 17 nm goes from 17 of 40 to 35 of 40, in four groups of 5-6; in it01 it stays at 13-24 of 40 and never exceeds groups of 5. The stuck complexes wander less (12 nm against 16 nm) and stretch by up to 24 %.
5. **A sheet of sticky MPM patches is a membrane for 1,000 frames** (it02): 85 patches and 10 proteins seeded as one continuous lattice, contact tension -0.2 between neighbours, hold between a 26 % crowd and a 10 % outside; no body crosses, the sheet's height spreads by 1.8 nm. The defect: the same tension stretches the proteins through the sheet to 1.28-1.47 of their radius of gyration (Young's modulus 20 for every body). it06 stiffens the proteins alone.
7. **A calmer bath reads better and costs little wander** (it05 against it01): halving every body's thermal speed and remembering its velocity four times longer lowers the mean change of a pixel between two movie frames from 16.4 to 11.6 of 255, while the RMS wander falls only from 13-33 nm to 10-26 nm. In the crowd the wander is set by collisions, so it follows the speed, not the bath's free diffusion (which the change kept).
8. **The membrane proteins' smear is not elastic strain** (it06 against it02): 5x the Young's modulus changes their final radius of gyration from 1.28-1.47 to 1.18-1.41 of the start, where a strain set by tension over stiffness would have fallen to a fifth. The contact tension -0.2 that glues them to the patches pulls their surface points along the sheet; nothing holds a body's own surface. it07 gives every body a surface tension of 0.3, above the contact tension.
9. **A body's own surface tension is not usable at this resolution** (smoke of 2026-11-14 21:05, not a record step): with `cell_surface_tension` 0.3 on every body the radius of gyration falls without end, the smallest bodies fastest (frame 60: 27 points 0.73 of the start, 64 points 0.89, 125 points 0.94-0.96, 343 points 0.98), and a 5x stiffer kind shrinks like the rest. At one point per grid cell a body's points can move together inside a cell unseen by the grid's strain. TRAPS T-103. So it07 lowers the glue instead.
10. **A quarter of the glue keeps the membrane proteins whole** (it07 against it06): at a patch-to-protein contact tension of -0.05 the proteins' radius of gyration ends at 1.03-1.11 of its start (1.18-1.41 at -0.2) and they are not squeezed out by the patches closing round them (heights within 1.4 nm of the sheet's mean at frames 500 and 1,000). The patch-to-patch tension stays -0.2.
11. **The two picture gains add up** (it08 against it04 and it05): in the 90 nm deep slab the calmer bath lowers the mean change of a pixel between two movie frames from 18.7 to 13.0 of 255 (30 % less, the same share as it05's 16.4 -> 11.6 in the 52 nm slab) and the bare ground stays under 5 % of the frame (3.9 % at the last frame); the RMS wander falls from 14-35 nm to 12-29 nm. Every body keeps its radius of gyration (1.00). Cost unchanged: 2.9 s per frame.
12. **The membrane holds in the deep slab, and a low tension still creeps** (it09): a sheet 19 x 9 sites holds for 1,000 frames between the 26 % crowd and the 10 % outside, nothing crosses, the patches move 1.3 nm RMS. But the proteins through it, glued at -0.05, go on spreading slowly: their largest radius of gyration is 1.03 of its start at frame 248, 1.16 at frame 500 and 1.28 at frame 1,000 (it07, the 5-deep sheet: 1.11 at the end). A negative contact tension makes a body creep over its partner at a rate that falls with the tension but does not stop; -0.05 is slow enough for a 1,000-frame movie, not a rest state.
13. **Many-lobed bodies hold; a chain held by a contact tension does not last** (it10): every free kind keeps its radius of gyration at 1.00 of its start for 1,000 frames, whatever its shape (four lobes, two lobes, a ring, a curved rod, a Y). The chains are another matter. Held by a tension of -0.05 between their own subunits they are whole at frame 100 (filament subunits at most 1.07) and degrade after: filament subunits at most 1.60 at frame 200 and 1.68 at the end, thread elements 1.60 at frame 300, 2.0 at frame 500 and 3.4 from frame 800 (a 27-point element pulled into pieces), bonds between 0.53 and 1.9 of their start. A 60-frame smoke cannot see this: both smokes of it10 were clean. The heads bound to the filaments at -0.05 creep to 1.33. So: a tension is for short contacts between whole bodies; what must stay joined for the whole movie needs a bond with a rest length.
14. **A stalked receptor holds in the sheet and collects its partner** (it11): the receptor built from stacked layers (a head wider than its site, a neck, a part that fills the site, a stub) keeps its shape for 1,000 frames (0.99-1.03 of its radius of gyration) and its height (3.3 to 5.2 nm above the sheet's middle, 6.0 seeded), where the plain channel beside it creeps to 1.37 at the same patch-to-protein tension. With a contact tension of -0.03 between Y body and receptor, 24 of 45 Y bodies end within 16 nm of a receptor (3 at the start), half of them within the first quarter of the run, and 15 of the 16 receptors carry one; the Y bodies stretch by at most 8 %.
15. **Spring bonds hold a chain where a tension does not** (it12 against it10, the same cast): with springs between the facing points of consecutive subunits and no tension, bond lengths stay within 0.90-1.09 of their start to frame 400 (it10: up to 1.9 by frame 200), filament subunits end at most 1.24 of their radius of gyration (1.68), heads 1.10 (1.33), and no thread is shredded (it10: elements at 3.5).
